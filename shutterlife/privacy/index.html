ShutterLife Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 27, 2026

Overview

ShutterLife is a camera shutter count reader for photographers, available for iPhone, iPad and Mac. This policy explains what data ShutterLife processes. The app works without an internet connection and sends nothing to us or to anyone else on its own. We only receive personal data when you write to us, for example through the app's support email (see "Contacting Us" and the two support sections).

Data Collection

The app transmits no data on its own. What reaches us is what you send by email yourself (see "Contacting Us" and the two support sections below). Because the app can prepare that email for you and attach a file, the App Privacy details on the App Store list Email Address, Customer Support, Photos or Videos and Other Data as data linked to you, for the purpose of App Functionality. This refers to exactly these emails and their attachments.

  • No account, no sign-in, no personal data gathered by the app for itself or for us
  • The app itself uploads or transmits no photos or image files
  • The app transmits no camera data (model, serial number, shutter count); it leaves your device only if you export a report yourself or email us a photo or its camera data
  • No usage analytics or crash reports are collected
  • No advertising identifiers are used
  • No cookies or tracking technologies are used
  • No location or GPS data is read, even when such data is present in the EXIF metadata of your photos

Exception: if you voluntarily email us photos or their camera data for firmware verification (see "Support Requests with Camera Photos" and "Support Requests with Camera Data"), they leave your device only when you send the email yourself. The app can attach them to the mail draft for you after asking, but it never sends anything itself.

Data Processing

All image file processing (EXIF parsing, shutter count extraction) happens entirely on your device. The app itself sends no data to any server.

Data Stored On Your Device

  • Shutter count readings: Camera model, shutter count, and date of each reading
  • Reading history: Previous readings stored locally for tracking the shutter count over time
  • Camera serial number (where present in EXIF): Stored to disambiguate multiple cameras of the same model.
  • Firmware version and EXIF capture date (where present in EXIF): Stored to correlate shutter count history with camera firmware version and original capture time.
  • File name of the file that was read: Stored and shown in the history, on the overview and in the report; included when you export a CSV or PDF.
  • Camera names: names you give your cameras, cameras you create by hand, and which reading belongs to which camera.
  • Last folder used: so that the file picker opens there again (a reference to the file you last picked and its folder).
  • Settings: Appearance (System, Light, Dark), how the history is grouped, which tips and hints you have already seen and whether onboarding is complete.
  • Rating prompt: three numbers so that the app asks you for an App Store rating only rarely: how many readings have succeeded, how often the app has been launched and when it last asked. No identifier, nothing about you; these numbers never leave your device and are deleted with the app. Whether the prompt appears is also up to your system, which limits it to a few per year.
  • Shared photos (iPhone and iPad): When you share several photos from another app to ShutterLife, the share extension places copies in a folder that only ShutterLife and its extension can access. The app reads them the next time it opens or returns to the foreground and then deletes the copies. Copies that are never picked up are deleted by the app the next time it opens once they are older than 24 hours.
  • Copies for a support email: If the app cannot read a photo, or you choose photos for us under "Report a Camera", it keeps unchanged copies in its temporary folder so that it can attach them, or the camera-data files it makes from them, to your email to us if you want. The copies and those files are deleted once you have written or discarded the email or cancelled, at the latest the next time the app starts.
  • Short-lived copies: photos you pick from the Photos app are copied to the app's temporary folder for reading and deleted afterwards; reports and result cards you share are created there too and deleted at the latest the next time the app starts.

The app stores the history, camera names, settings, the last used folder and the temporary copies because the features you use need them (Section 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG, the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act). The three numbers for the rating prompt do not serve a feature you asked for; that is why we name them here openly. This data is only accessible by the ShutterLife app, is never transmitted automatically to any external service, and is deleted when you uninstall the app. On the Mac, the app's data folder remains until you delete it yourself. It only leaves your device if you yourself export a report (PDF, CSV or plain text) or a result card (image) via the Share Sheet or send us a support email, and even then only to the destination you actively select while sharing (e.g. Mail, Notes, AirDrop). You can delete readings and camera names at any time in the app (History > Delete All). Everything else, including settings, goes when you delete the app.

Storage duration: Readings persist on your device indefinitely until you explicitly delete them via History > Delete All or uninstall the app. On the Mac, the app's data folder remains until you delete it yourself. There is no automatic deletion, since the data lives entirely under your control on your device.

Internet Connection

The app itself does not transmit any data to remote servers. It functions entirely offline.

Saving a Result Card to Photos (iPhone and iPad)

If you choose "Save Image" in the iOS share sheet for a result card, iOS may ask you to allow ShutterLife to add pictures to your Photos (write-only access). ShutterLife cannot read, list or change the pictures in your photo library, and it receives nothing back. What you save stays on your device (or syncs through your own iCloud Photos settings with Apple).

Third-Party Services

ShutterLife does not integrate with any third-party analytics, advertising, or data collection services.

Hosting (GitHub Pages)

This website is hosted by GitHub, Inc. When the pages are accessed, GitHub processes technically necessary server log data (including IP address and time of access) to ensure secure and reliable delivery of the pages (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We ourselves have no access to these server logs. GitHub is self-certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and additionally relies on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses; details are governed by GitHub's own privacy policy.

Distribution and Apple

ShutterLife is distributed through Apple's App Store. Apple may collect diagnostic, usage and download metadata independently from this app under Apple's own privacy policy. ShutterLife has no access to that data and is not the controller for Apple's processing.

International data transfers: Apple Inc. is headquartered in the United States. According to Apple, transfers of diagnostic and usage metadata to Apple's US infrastructure are based on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR, Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914). Since ShutterLife has no access to that data flow, Apple acts as an independent controller under its own privacy policy.

Aggregated App Store Connect analytics: ShutterLife may receive aggregated, anonymized analytics (e.g., installation counts, active devices, retention) from Apple's App Store Connect, provided users have opted in to "Share with App Developers" in their device settings. These reports contain no user identifiers and cannot be traced back to individuals.

Legal Basis

What the app stores on your device stays there and does not reach us; we do not process it. We only process personal data when you write to us. The legal bases are stated under "Contacting Us", the two support sections and "Hosting".

Contacting Us

If you contact us by email or phone (contact details in the Legal Notice), we process the information you provide (e.g., email address, name, content of your inquiry) solely to handle your request. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in responding to your inquiry). If you voluntarily attach files, such as photos, the legal basis for those is your consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, which you may withdraw at any time with effect for the future. Our mailbox is hosted by IONOS SE as our processor in the EU; details under "Recipient" in the next section. Storage duration: We delete the data once your request has been fully handled, and in any case no later than 90 days thereafter, unless statutory retention obligations apply. For attachments (photos or camera data), the shorter periods of the two support sections below apply instead of the 90 days.

You are under no legal or contractual obligation to give us any data. Without your email address we cannot reply; without a photo or its camera data we cannot check whether your camera's counter can be read. You can use the app fully without any of this.

Support Requests with Camera Photos (Firmware Verification)

If your camera model or firmware version is not yet supported, the app offers a contact path in the error message; you will also find it under "About", and the App Store description names it too. The contact button opens a pre-filled mail draft to support@casystudio.com. The app fills in the subject and, depending on the reason, the camera model, firmware version or file name, plus a sentence saying you send the attachments for this check only; for "Send Feedback", the app version. You can change or delete all of it before sending. If the app could not read a photo, it first asks what to attach: by default a small file with the camera data from exactly that photo ("Attach Camera Data", see "Support Requests with Camera Data"), or the photo itself, unchanged ("Attach Original Photo Instead"); where no camera-data file can be made from a format, it offers the photo only ("Attach File"). If you checked several photos at once, this applies to each photo concerned. Through "Report a Camera" (under "About") or "Camera not listed? Report a Camera" (below a reading), you first choose one or two original photos from Photos or Files. The app only receives the photos you pick, and then asks in the same way what to attach. If no account is set up in Apple Mail, the app offers to hand the camera-data file through the share menu to an app you choose (for example Gmail or Outlook); you enter the address there yourself, and ShutterLife puts it at the start of the shared text. On a Mac, if the app cannot hand the file to your mail app, it shows the file in the Finder ("Show in Finder and Write Email") and opens the email beside it, so you attach the file yourself. You can instead choose "Write Email Without File" and decide in your mail app whether and which photo you attach. The app itself sends nothing: you see the draft with its attachment, can change it, add more photos or discard it, and only your own send sends it.

Purpose: We evaluate the technical capture data of the photos you send us to check whether ShutterLife can read your camera's shutter count with your firmware version, and to add support for it.

These photos may contain personal data beyond the image itself — for example your camera's serial number, capture timestamps, and, if enabled on your camera, GPS coordinates. If the photo shows people, this applies to their data too. The legal basis is your consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR. You give it by agreeing to the attachment when the app asks, or by deliberately attaching the photo yourself, and sending the email. You may withdraw it at any time with effect for the future, informally by email to the same address; we will then delete the files without delay. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before it.

Where a submitted photo shows people, we process their data on the basis of Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in handling your support request); we only evaluate the technical metadata, not the image content. Where possible, please send photos without people; any subject will do.

Recipient: Your email and its attachments are received and stored at IONOS SE, Elgendorfer Straße 57, 56410 Montabaur, Germany. IONOS acts as our processor under Art. 28 GDPR. Under that agreement, processing takes place within the European Union or the European Economic Area; the sub-processors used for our mailbox are located in Germany (as of our check in August 2026). No transfer to third countries is envisaged through this mailbox; for Mail Drop, see the next paragraph. Your email first passes through your own mail account or mail app (on the Mac, any mail app you have set as default), under that provider's terms. If you share the file through the share menu, it passes through the app you choose there, under that app's terms. ShutterLife does not control that route and sends nothing itself.

Large attachments via Mail Drop: If your attachments are too large for your mail service, Apple Mail may offer to send them with Mail Drop; this requires being signed in to iCloud. If you accept, Apple uploads the attachments to iCloud, and instead of the files we receive a link that we use to download them from Apple's servers. Mail Drop is a service Apple provides to you, not to us: Apple stores the files under its own terms and privacy policy. For users in the EU, the controller is Apple Distribution International Ltd., Ireland; according to Apple, personal data is generally stored by Apple Inc. in the United States, and Apple bases such transfers on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. According to Apple, Mail Drop attachments expire after 30 days. We have no control over that copy and cannot delete it earlier. Files we download through the link are handled like any other attachment: used only for the check and deleted as described below. If you'd rather not use Mail Drop, choose "Attach Camera Data" instead; that file is small.

We use the photos you send us exclusively to verify your camera's firmware. We do not share them with any third party and do not use them as test data without first asking your explicit permission. Storage duration: We delete the photos once the check is complete, and in any case no later than 30 days after your email arrives, unless you expressly allow us to keep them for longer. This section supplements the general "Contacting Us" section for the photo attachments of this specific support path.

Transition period for older app versions: Up to and including version 1.0.7, our former support address was configured in the app: shutterlife.support@gmail.com. That mailbox is a free Google account; our contracting party is Google Ireland Limited, Dublin. Google offers no data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR for such accounts, and Google may transfer messages to servers of Google LLC in the United States. Google LLC is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (European Commission adequacy decision (EU) 2023/1795); transfers to the United States rely on it (Art. 45 GDPR; certification checked on October 20, 2026). We still read that mailbox so that requests from older app versions are not lost, and delete the messages there once they have been handled. Please do not send photos to that address; use support@casystudio.com only. We will shut the old channel down on October 31, 2026.

Support Requests with Camera Data

When the app offers to write to us with a file — after a photo could not be read, under "Report a Camera" or "Camera not listed? Report a Camera", or for a file in a purchase check — it attaches by default a small file with your camera's data instead of the photo. The file is made on your device from your photo: the picture and its previews are removed, and so are the GPS position, the usual name and comment fields (artist, copyright, owner name, descriptions, comments, XMP and IPTC data) and the world-time cities some cameras record. A name that a camera keeps in a private manufacturer field may remain. What stays: camera model, serial numbers of body and lens, firmware version, capture time with its time-zone offset, exposure and lens data, and the shutter counters. Together with your email address, the serial number and capture time can identify you, so we treat the file as personal data.

The legal basis is your consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, which you give by choosing "Attach Camera Data" and sending the email, or by choosing "Share Camera Data" (on a Mac, "Show in Finder and Write Email") and sending the file to us; you may withdraw it at any time as described above. We use the file only to check your camera, do not share it with any third party, and delete it within 30 days of your email arriving, unless you expressly allow us to keep it for longer. Recipient and transfer are as for photos (section above). ShutterLife sends nothing itself; the email leaves your device only when you send it. If you choose "Attach Original Photo Instead", the photo goes unchanged and carries everything a photo does; then the section above applies.

Children's Privacy

ShutterLife is not directed at children. The app gathers no data about its users; for email inquiries, the sections above apply.

Your Rights Under GDPR

Under the GDPR, you have the following rights with respect to personal data processed by us: access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), and objection (Art. 21). Outside the sections "Contacting Us", "Support Requests with Camera Photos" and "Support Requests with Camera Data", we do not collect or store any personal data; in that respect there is no personal data for you to request, modify, or delete from us. All app data remains exclusively on your device under your full control. Readings and camera names can be removed via "History > Delete All", all other app data by deleting the app. On the Mac, the app's data folder remains until you delete it yourself. For data from contact and support requests, these rights apply against us until the data is deleted under the retention periods stated above.

Right to object: Where we process data on the basis of our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR: answering your inquiry, people shown in submitted photos, the website's server logs), you may object at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation. An informal email to support@casystudio.com is enough. We will then stop processing the data unless we can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds.

ShutterLife does not engage in automated decision-making, including profiling (Art. 22 GDPR).
